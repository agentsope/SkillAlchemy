# Seven video-type application guides

These are authored application guides required by C04. They help fill the C03 director schema from the user’s goal; they are not seven empirically validated genre formulas. Load [prompt-patterns.md](prompt-patterns.md), then [director-playbook.md](director-playbook.md). Select an engine by actual eligibility and availability in [engine-adapters.md](engine-adapters.md), not by genre alone. All new examples or shot skeletons are synthetic demonstrations.

## 1. Product launch and SaaS promotion

**Trigger:** the user wants to explain, introduce or promote a product/action. Collect product identity, audience, requested action, real UI/assets, supplied claims, target placement and rights. Keep fictional metrics visibly fictional.

**Build:** state what product behavior must be understood; choose shots that show the user-requested action; attach proof claims only where supplied or sourced; fill the shot/timeline/style ledger. A synthetic project can describe an initial state → visible authorized product action → resulting state → requested ending, but this is an authored application of the brief, not a proven launch formula.

**Optional scoped material:** P07’s fictional Tidy film embodies a sorting action with one cursor; it is one 42-second dark-keynote demo, not the style’s default. P06’s optional Bright Hook→Problem→Solution→Proof/Demo→CTA guidance applies only to 90–180-second structured pieces teaching/selling one idea and explicitly excludes social clips, trailers, loops and isolated hero scenes.

**Prompt fill:** “For <audience>, show <verified product action> using <authorized assets>. Preserve <claims with provenance>, <duration/platform> and <user constraints>. Implement shots <table>; any missing UI/statistics remain <unknown/synthetic>. End with <user-requested action>. Route by <eligible adapter>.”

**Review:** verify the depicted UI/action and claim status, timeline closure, literal copy and intended ending. Do not report successful persuasion from rendering or sampled frames. See [synthetic product application](../examples/product-launch.md).

## 2. Vertical TikTok, Reels and Shorts

**Trigger:** the requested output is a vertical social placement. Record the actual platform and whether it is organic or advertising, aspect, duration, supplied footage/copy and audio intent. Treat platform overlays as placement-specific unknowns until established.

**Build:** compose a shot table within the requested portrait frame, with explicit subject and caption regions. Choose the narrative from the communicated message and assets. Do not import P06’s long structured explainer arc into this excluded scope.

**Scoped layout notes:** P13’s portrait top-band branch applies only to its recorded legacy/Cinematic interview-caption module. P15 requires the matching official safe-zone template only for TikTok auction in-feed advertising with the stated overlay/anchor configuration. Neither supplies an organic/Reels/Shorts universal pixel margin.

**Prompt fill:** “Produce <placement> at <requested aspect/duration>. Show <message/action> with <shot table>. Keep <literal copy> in the project’s declared caption region. Apply a platform template only when <exact applicable placement/template> is available; otherwise report the missing overlay contract. Audio intent is <state>.”

**Review:** inspect actual caption/subject overlap and placement preview when available; label preview/device limitations. Language substitution and silent-comprehension contrasts were not demonstrated.

## 3. Data visualization and science

**Trigger:** the user wants viewers to understand data or an explanatory mechanism. Collect dataset/source, units, domain, known/unknown values, claimed conclusion, audience, audio intent and literal labels.

**Build:** distinguish measured data from illustrative placeholders. Fill marks/scales/labels in the director plan from the available facts; choose reveal order as an authored project decision. Keep unknown observations visibly unknown rather than inventing values.

**Scoped material:** P08’s temperature film moves from individual observations to a full-series reveal and unknown future cell; this is its particular selected narrative. P05 is optional guidance for 2–10-minute word-led explainers whose pictures follow words; it does not prove short/social or silent-video comprehension. P09’s note-density ratio belongs only to its dataviz style.

**Prompt fill:** “Explain <question> using <dataset/mechanism source>. Preserve <units and literal labels>, identify <unknown/synthetic data>, and implement <shot/mark/reveal table>. Do not imply an unsourced factual endpoint or interpolate values as factual claims. Narration and captions use <actual measured/provisional clock>.”

**Review:** check labels, source notes, literal numerals, unknown cells and factual consistency. P33/P34 are historical eCPM-film repairs, not global reading floors or a ban on clearly labeled illustrative interpolation. See [synthetic data application](../examples/data-explainer.md).

## 4. Kinetic typography, logo and brand animation

**Trigger:** the user requests motion type, a logo reveal or brand sequence. Collect exact brand copy, approved logo/font/color assets, intended action, duration and any beat/audio constraints.

**Build:** fill each text/logo state, focal geometry, transition intent and required arrival/end state. Preserve literal lettering and asset rights. Select motion values as explicit project choices rather than universal easing/spring defaults.

**Scoped material:** P11’s zoom/iris/wipe choice follows the outgoing focus of one 17-second kinetic-reel code demo and shared cues. Its Canvas/WebGL encoder is source-specific P27, not a generic fallback for all brand animation. P03’s named banknote payoff is confined to its stat-countup revision.

**Prompt fill:** “Animate <literal approved logo/copy> through <state/shot table> for <brand intent>. Required geometry/layout is <values>; delegated aesthetic choices are <assumptions>. Align <declared cues> only to available audio. Keep the ending <explicit requirement>.”

**Review:** verify literal type/logo appearance, required payoff and motion evidence. A still cannot establish pacing, and code inspection cannot establish a watched result.

## 5. Cinematic trailer and visual short

**Trigger:** the user requests a cinematic visual story, short or trailer. Collect story/action, characters, authorized footage or synthetic imagery intent, required event/payoff, duration, palette/camera constraints and audio intent.

**Build:** write the requested story as shot/action/framing/transition/timing fields. Preserve required actions and outcomes in the plan. Camera and continuity choices must come from the user’s intent or explicit authored assumptions.

**Limited scoped material:** P12 is an eleven-second two-shot fallen-star painted-animation code demo: night palette, brush wipe, character-targeted iris and camera opening during star flight. It was not rendered in the audit and does not establish a general cinematic/trailer grammar. P06 explicitly excludes trailers. Coverage of cinematic production is limited.

**Prompt fill:** “Tell <user story> in <duration> with shots <table>. Required event and payoff are <literal actions>. Camera/continuity decisions are <stated or assumed values>. Use only <authorized assets/eligible programmatic adapter>. Report implementation/render/review state separately.”

**Review:** inspect required actions, continuity and actual full motion/audio when available. Do not claim cinematic success from code or isolated images. Diffusion remains the separate extension below.

## 6. Website, GitHub project or documentation to video

**Trigger:** the user wants a video derived from public/provided web or documentation material. Collect the exact source/revision, target audience, intended explanation/action, approved screenshots/assets, source claims and output requirements.

**Build:** extract claims with provenance into the brief, preserve unknowns, then write a shot/script table that represents the requested content. Website/repository availability does not transfer media/font/music permissions. C03 supplies this schema; no acquired matched contrast validates one universal docs-to-video sequence.

**Optional material:** P05 can apply only when the result meets its 2–10-minute word-led narrated-explainer scope. P06 can apply only within its stated structured 90–180-second scope. P28 distinguishes narration-ready timing from pending placeholders; it does not authorize a paid TTS service.

**Prompt fill:** “Turn <source/revision> into <requested video> for <audience>. Use claims <provenance list>, authorized assets <ledger>, and shots <table>. Preserve unsupported facts as unknown. Narration is <ready/pending/intentionally silent>; route through <eligible adapter>.”

**Review:** compare factual wording to its source, inspect UI/copy legibility and retain source/revision in the handoff. See [synthetic narrated-docs application](../examples/narrated-docs.md).

## 7. Voice-led knowledge explanation

**Trigger:** spoken explanation drives the requested film. Collect approved script/facts, audience, authorized audio or unresolved supply, language, desired duration and captions.

**Build:** when ready audio and measured timing exist, derive the project’s scene/subtitle tables from that clock (P28’s source workflow). With pending audio, mark timing/alignment provisional and deliver the actual plan/code state. Preserve unit/index conventions at adapter translation. Do not represent an empty subtitle array or 300-frame placeholder as completed narration.

**Optional material:** P05’s single question/journey and motivation-before-mechanism guidance remains pending validation within 2–10-minute word-led explainers. P06 applies only to its separate 90–180-second structured scope. P29’s boundary/interpolation behavior, P30’s fixed-frame retiming and P31’s Motion Canvas named events remain separate implementation contexts.

**Prompt fill:** “Explain <question> to <audience> from <facts>. Narration source/state is <authorized ready audio plus timing | pending>. Implement shots/captions against <measured or provisional clock>, preserving <units and range conventions>. If voice/script/rate changes, identify affected cues and apply only the selected workflow’s retiming contract.”

**Review:** listen to the actual final audio and view the actual moving caption/scene boundaries before reporting sync or intelligibility. Narration intent keeps `audio_listened` unresolved/not_executed when audio is absent; missing audio is not intentional silence.

## Separate optional diffusion extension — authored C09

Do not import code-engine clocks, registration, resource readiness or export contracts into a diffusion prompt, and do not fabricate model-specific rules.
